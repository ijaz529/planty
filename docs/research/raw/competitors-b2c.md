# Raw notes — B2C plant subscription & rental competitors

Research lens: consumer-facing plant subscription / rental companies and what happened to them.
Date of research: 2026-09-09. Every number carries its source URL and the year the source reports / was fetched.
Labels: **[reported]** = stated by the linked source; **[estimate]** = my own inference; **[anecdotal]** = review/forum/blog.

Method note: ~30 WebSearch queries (EN + DE), then WebFetch of ~60 competitor, review and news pages. Reddit was not fetchable from this environment; several domains (Wikipedia, Companies House, livelyroot.com, miet24.de, bloomboxclub.com, feey.ch, botanicly.de) were blocked by the fetch policy mid-session, so a few claims rely on search snippets and are marked accordingly.

---

## 1. The big picture (what the evidence says)

1. **"Plant-in-a-box" monthly subscriptions cluster at USD 15–69 / GBP 30–38 / EUR ~39 per month for ONE plant (usually with a pot), shipped by parcel.** Nobody in the consumer segment charges for ongoing *care*; the "subscription" is a recurring purchase, not a rental.
2. **The consumer rental-with-care model for homes essentially does not exist at scale in any of the three markets researched (US/UK, DE/NL, UAE).** The one documented attempt at a home plant rental/rent-to-own model in Berlin (Horticure, 2020, from EUR 20/month) is defunct. All "Pflanzen mieten" / "plant hire" providers found in DE, NL, UK and UAE are B2B or event-only, typically with 6–12 month minimum terms and ~EUR 150–200/month entry price points.
3. **Two of the best-known consumer plant subscription brands died in 2023–2025 (Horti, US, closed 31 May 2025; The Plant Box, DE, insolvent 28 Jul 2023).** The Sill quietly turned its subscription into a 3/6-month prepaid club with no skip/cancel. Bloomscape has never offered a subscription and has a 2.8/5 Trustpilot. The survivors (Patch, Beards & Daisies, feey, Plantsome) are essentially e-commerce retailers with strong reviews, not subscription businesses.
4. **Top complaints across every Trustpilot profile are identical: transit damage (soil spill, broken leaves, upside-down parcels), late delivery, pests on arrival, and "value" (same plant cheaper at the supermarket).** None of those problems exist in a hand-delivered, technician-maintained rental model — but that model carries a very different cost structure (labour, vehicles, 6–12-month minimums in every B2B example).
5. **Recurly's July-2026 benchmark puts average B2C e-commerce subscription churn at ~4.25% (median annual churn rate as reported) vs 3.22% for B2B SaaS; voluntary churn is the majority everywhere.** Plant-box anecdotes show much faster cancellation: reviewers cancelling after 2 months because the value did not hold up.

## 4. Churn / retention signals
- Recurly benchmark (July 2026 data): overall churn 3.60% (2.34% voluntary + 1.25% involuntary); B2B SaaS 3.22%; B2C e-commerce "4.25% median annual churn rate" with higher voluntary churn reflecting "lower price points and impulse-driven signup patterns" — https://recurly.com/research/churn-rate-benchmarks/ (2026). (The page labels the e-commerce figure "annual"; other Recurly figures are typically monthly — treat magnitude with care.)
- Plant-box specific **[anecdotal]**: Horti reviewer cancelled after 2 months (value); Plantsolve author's cancel triggers = duplicates and grocery-store equivalents; Leaf Envy complaint about auto-billing after a gift sub ended; The Sill and Horti both moved to "no skip / no cancel once charged" rules — a sign that skip/cancel behaviour was hurting them **[estimate]**.
- No company publishes subscriber counts or churn. Bloombox Club DE claims 200,000 plants delivered since 2015 (~18k/yr **[estimate]**), and has paused its DE shop (2026).

## 5. What breaks logistically (from reviews and closures)
1. Parcel transit: soil spill, snapped stems, upside-down boxes, cold damage (Horti ships heat packs, Mon–Wed only in winter). Present in 100% of the Trustpilot profiles read (Patch, The Sill, Bloomscape, Lively Root, Leaf Envy, Beards & Daisies).
2. Pests on arrival (spider mites, gnats) — Beards & Daisies Sept 2026, Bloomscape.
3. Stock-outs discovered after the promised date (Lively Root Mar 2026).
4. Missing care info / gift notes (House Plant Box, Bloomscape).
5. Margin compression from energy + purchasing costs (The Plant Box, DE 2023: <50% GM → insolvency).
6. Founder burnout in a low-margin, high-touch category (Horti 2025).

## 6. Lessons extracted for Planty
- Consumers demonstrably pay **€30–40 / £30–38 / $28–69 per month** for one plant they *keep*. A rental where they keep nothing must therefore deliver something else: zero maintenance, zero dead plants, seasonal swaps, and larger statement plants that are impractical to buy/ship.
- The only price evidence for full-service (delivery + care visits + replacements) is B2B: **€85/month for 5–8 plants with bi-weekly visits (Canal Plants, Amsterdam, 2026)**, €150–200/month entry with 6–12-month minimums (Leafy Life, Plantclub, WELO), or ~€14/plant/month (AS Hydroplant). Weekly visits at consumer scale are not offered by anyone found; bi-weekly (or 4-weekly in DE) is the norm.
- Every rental provider mitigates route-density risk with minimum terms (6–12 months) and service-area limits (one metro). A "2-week rental" consumer product has no precedent outside event hire (€5–60/plant/day NL; €36/plant/3 days DE).
- Berlin already had a €20/month home-rental attempt (Horticure) fail, and a €39/month plant-Abo (Bloombox DE) pause; Germany's most successful plant startup (feey) sells rather than rents. Dubai has incumbents already serving villas with maintenance but zero price transparency and quote-based sales.
