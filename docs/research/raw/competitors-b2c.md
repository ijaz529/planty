# Lens: B2C plant subscription & rental competitors

Research date: 2026-09-10. Every number below carries a year and a URL. Where I could
not find a figure I say so explicitly. "Estimate" marks my own inference; everything
else is reported by the cited source.

---

## 0. Headline finding first

**Nobody is running a successful, venture-scale B2C *rental* (as opposed to *sale* or
*box subscription*) business for houseplants in homes.** Across DE, NL, UK, US and UAE
I found:

- A large, old, quote-only **B2B** plant-rental industry (interiorscaping) with real,
  boring, profitable unit economics.
- A wave of **B2C plant e-commerce / subscription-box** startups from 2018–2021, of
  which a striking number are now dead, liquidated, or visibly distressed.
- Almost **zero pure-play B2C plant rental** for private homes. The closest real
  example is Reflower (NL) at €30–45/month, and that is a small operator, not a startup.

That asymmetry is itself the most important input to Planty's MVP decision.

## 1. B2C plant subscription boxes — the graveyard

### 1.1 Horti (US) — CLOSED May 2025

- Homepage banner (fetched 2026-09-10): "WE have closed Horti as of May 31, 2025 and
  not accepting new orders." — https://heyhorti.com/
- Closure mechanics from their FAQ: last subscription processing **May 15**, shop fully
  closed **June 1**, support inbox open until **June 30**; prepaid/gift subscribers got
  "all remaining plants in a single shipment."
  — https://heyhorti.com/pages/help-center-faqs
- Stated reason is *personal*, not explicitly financial: founders cite "young families
  and seeking a bit more stability"; one founder took a sabbatical, the other "returned
  to tech work." Their framing: "it feels truest to let Horti complete its natural cycle
  of birth, flourishing, and closure."
  — https://heyhorti.com/pages/help-center-faqs
- Pricing while alive: **$28.00/month**, US-only shipping, one 6" plant (or 2–3 smaller)
  plus a hand-painted pot. Plans were "Month to Month", "6 month story", "12 month
  journey."
  — https://www.mysubscriptionaddiction.com/b/horti (rating 4.3/5, only 3 user ratings)
- Top user criticism, verbatim: "The plants we got were underwhelming, and both plants
  came in the exact same generic terracotta pot" — reviewer compared $28/month against
  "$12 for two" at a grocery store.
  — https://www.mysubscriptionaddiction.com/b/horti

**Lesson:** at $28/month a *shipped* plant box is directly price-anchored against the
supermarket plant shelf, and loses. Horti's own customers did that arithmetic in public.

### 1.4 The Plant Box (DE) — INSOLVENT July 2023

- Insolvency filed **28 July 2023**; founded 2020; revenue **~€1 million in 2022**;
  had taken **€150,000** from two "Die Höhle der Löwen" investors (Judith Williams,
  Carsten Maschmeyer).
  — https://www.myhomebook.de/news/the-plant-box-insolvent
- Reasons reported: rising energy prices, purchasing costs that could only be partly
  passed on, handmade production (product prices from ~€80), falling discretionary

  — https://www.myhomebook.de/news/the-plant-box-insolvent
- Founders' closing line: "Unfortunately, not every dream comes true."

**Lesson (DE-specific):** two German plant-subscription/DTC-plant businesses failed
inside 12 months of each other (2023 and 2024), in a market where consumer discretionary
spend was contracting. Anything Planty does in Germany has to survive that same
sensitivity.

### 1.6 Lively Root (US) — quietly moved from monthly to QUARTERLY

- All live subscriptions are **quarterly**, not monthly:
  Easy Care **$42** (from $55), Pet Friendly **$49** (from $58), Not-So-Common **$49**
  (from $58); Gift subscription **from $109** prepaid for 4 quarterly deliveries.
  — https://www.livelyroot.com/pages/lively-root-subscriptions (2026)
- Their own justification for quarterly: it keeps deliveries "manageable" and seasonal
  "without overwhelming recipients." Cancellation allowed **after two successful
  shipments**.
  — https://www.livelyroot.com/pages/lively-root-subscriptions

**Lesson:** the industry's own answer to "why do people cancel a plant box?" is
*saturation* — a home only absorbs so many pots. Lively Root's fix was to cut cadence by
3x. Planty's rotate-and-swap model is structurally better here, because a swap does not
increase the customer's plant count.

- Headcount **-26% year over year** per Growjo (undated snapshot — treat as soft).
  — https://growjo.com/company/Bloomscape
- Trustpilot: **2.8/5 over 100 reviews**, **45% 1-star**, only 4 reviews in the last

  — https://www.trustpilot.com/review/bloomscape.com
- Verbatim: "The plant was completely destroyed" (2022-05-10); "Plants arrived with

  — https://www.trustpilot.com/review/bloomscape.com

### 1.8 Patch Plants (UK) — the counter-example that proves the point

- Trustpilot **4.7/5 over 15,920 reviews**, 93% 5-star, 692 reviews in the last
  12 months (2026). Patch replies to **94% of negative reviews**, usually within 24h.
  — https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/patchplants.com
- Residual complaints are *courier* complaints, not plant complaints: "DPD threw my plant
  and then slammed it down upside down" (2026-08-26); "The carrier made a bit of a dogs
  breakfast with the packaging" (2026-08-31).
  — https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/patchplants.com
- Corporate: Patch (Patch Gardens Ltd) raised only **$2.97M over 3 rounds from 28
  investors** and was **acquired by Arena Flowers**; 2024 filings show a **cash decrease
  of -23.75%** year on year.
  — https://tracxn.com/d/companies/patch/__dkSkDVCm-sZEN68ngj9Pr-S6KLqjTfMZXhlAMboYNvw
  — https://uk.globaldatabase.com/company/patch-gardens-ltd
- Patch's **business/office** offering exists but is explicitly **quote-only**: "bespoke
  installations for businesses as well as plant maintenance", routed to
  business@patchplants — no published price.
  — https://www.patchplants.com/pages/businesses/

**Lesson:** Bloomscape 2.8 vs Patch 4.7 is the cleanest natural experiment in this
dataset. Both sell plants online. Bloomscape ships plants long-distance by parcel across
the US; Patch grew up doing dense local delivery in London. **The delta is logistics, not
brand.** Planty's model (local van, delivery + install by a human) sits on the *good*
side of that line by construction — but only within one dense city.

## 2. Actual B2C *rental* — the thin evidence

### 2.1 Reflower (NL) — the only clean B2C home-rental price I found

- **L: €30/month rental. XL: €45/month rental.** (Buy-out alternatives €300 / €450.)
  — https://www.reflower.nl/collections/planten (2026)
- Terms: monthly invoice by email; "Opzeggen is iedere maand mogelijk met inachtname van
  een maand opzegtermijn" — **cancel any month with one month's notice**. Delivery only
  in Noord-Holland, Zuid-Holland and Utrecht, **on Wednesdays**.
  — https://www.reflower.nl/collections/planten
- Serves both **"Thuis" (home) and "Zakelijk" (business)**.

Note the operational shape: a **fixed weekly delivery day** in a **restricted region**.
That is route density being enforced at the product level. Planty should copy this.

### 2.2 Other NL price points (secondary, from search snippets — lower confidence)

- A NL cuttings subscription at **€19.99/month** (5 cuttings/month).
- Jouwplant: **from €29.95** monthly delivery up to **€84.95** yearly.
  — https://www.jouwplant.nl/planten-abonnement/ (2026, via search snippet — not fetched)

### 2.3 Germany: rental exists, but is B2B and quote-only

This was the most consistent finding of the whole lens. Every German "Pflanzen mieten"
provider I fetched sells to offices, on 12–24 month contracts, at prices you must phone
for.

- **Reiffer / objektbepflanzung.de** — the only German provider publishing a per-plant
  rate: "Mietpreise beginnen bei **9€ pro Pflanze (pro Monat)**"; "der Mindestmietbetrag
  pro Monat beträgt **75€**"; "Die Mindestmietdauer beträgt **2 Jahre**"; care visit
  **every three weeks** (watering, trimming, tying, cleaning); "Im Mietpreis ist alles
  enthalten" except VAT and water; dead plants replaced free.
  — https://objektbepflanzung.de/pflanzen-vermietung (2026)
- **WELO Green** — packages: Basic **ab €199/Monat** (≈5–10 plant concepts, offices to
  200 m²); Comfort **ab €299/Monat** (≈15–20 concepts, to 400 m²); Plus **ab €649/Monat**
  (to 1000 m², incl. plant wall). All: **care every 3 weeks**, free plant-guarantee
  replacement, **12-month minimum then monthly cancellable**. **No residential package.**
  — https://welo-green.de/pages/mietpflanzen and
    https://welo-green.de/blogs/journal/pflanzen-mieten-kosten (2026)
- **Rogel Raumbegrünung (hydrokultur.de)** — "Büro, Praxis & Empfang", "planbare,
  transparente Kosten", "monatliche Preise" — but **no prices published**; consultation
  request only.
  — https://hydrokultur.de/leistungen/mietpflanzen/ (2026)
- **Pflanzen-Kölle** and **Plantyworks** — same pattern, no published prices.
  — https://www.pflanzen-koelle.de/filialen/gaertnerservice/innenraumbegruenung/pflanzen-mieten/
  — https://plantyworks.de/berlin/
- A 2022 LifeVerde round-up of German rental/swap options lists Bekra (Munich), Gärtnerei
  Emil Schäfer (Stuttgart), Hydroplan (Cologne/Düsseldorf), Meyflower (Berlin/Bremen),
  Plantclub (Berlin), Oh Tannenbaum — and gives **no prices for any of them**. It also
  frames the consumer alternative bluntly: eBay Kleinanzeigen, Facebook plant-swap groups,
  and local swap markets, which cost **€0**.
  — https://www.lifeverde.de/nachhaltigkeitsmagazin/news-tipps/pflanzen-mieten-und-tauschen-statt-kaufen-von-berlin-bis-muenchen (2022)

### 2.4 Plantclub (Berlin) — closest thing to "Planty, but B2B", and the scale reality check

This is the single most decision-relevant competitor in the whole lens.

- Founded **2019**, HQ Berlin, "plants-as-a-service membership" model.
  — https://www.crunchbase.com/organization/plantclub
- Cities: **Berlin, Hamburg, Cologne, Düsseldorf, Frankfurt, Munich, Vienna** (7 cities).
  — https://plantclub.io/de/pflanzen-mieten-berlin (2026)
- Published, self-serve-looking pricing (rare in this industry):
  **Greenhouse €200/mo** (to 150 m²), **Woodland €400/mo** (to 500 m²),
  **Jungle €600/mo** (to 1,000 m²), **Rainforest** custom (1,000+ m²).
  All billed annually, ex-VAT, **12-month minimum term**, setup/installation included,
  free replacements, dedicated account management.
  — https://plantclub.io/en/pricing (2026)
- Care cadence: "Your dedicated plant expert visits **every two weeks**."
  — https://plantclub.io/de/pflanzen-mieten-berlin
- Scale: "**150+ Berlin members**"; logos claimed include Netflix, Figma, GetYourGuide.
  Company-wide they cite **over 100 members** earlier and **over 200 members in 2025**.
  — https://plantclub.io/de/pflanzen-mieten-berlin
  — https://www.plantclub.io/blog-en/plantclub-2025-plantclub-turns-5-a-green-journey
    (referenced via search result; the direct URL 404s as of 2026-09-10 — treat the
    "200 members" figure as reported-by-search-snippet, medium confidence)
- Funding: a **grant from BFF Berlin Founders Fund, 01 April 2020**; Silicon Allee listed
  as an investor. No priced venture round found.
  — https://app.dealroom.co/companies/plantclub_io

**Read this carefully.** After ~6 years, 7 cities, and a B2B-only focus, Plantclub is at
roughly **200 members**. At their €200–600/month tiers that implies order-of-magnitude
**€0.5M–1.4M ARR** (my estimate, from published tier prices × reported member count —
not a reported revenue figure). This is a solid small business. It is not a market where
a second entrant wins by being slightly nicer. It *is* evidence that the B2B rental
motion works, is defensible, and is being executed by exactly one venture-flavoured
player in Berlin — with **zero** equivalent player addressing homes.

## 3. B2B rental price benchmarks (for calibrating Planty's own pricing)

US interiorscape pricing, published 2026-08-03:

- Low-light plants (Sansevieria, ZZ): **$20–$35 per plant per month**
- Medium (Mass Cane, Dracaena): **$40–$75 per plant per month**
- Large specimens (Fiddle Leaf Fig, Bird of Paradise): **$90–$150 per plant per month**
- Complete programs: **$200–$1,200/month**; small offices (5–10 plants) **$100–$350/mo**;
  large floors (20–40 plants) **$500–$1,500/mo**
- **Minimum monthly charge typically $100–$200**
- Standard contract **12-month minimum with rolling renewals**; biweekly or monthly visits
- Rental over 36 months costs **30–50% more than outright purchase**, but includes
  maintenance and guaranteed replacement
  — https://officeplants.com/2026/08/03/office-plant-rental-pricing/

Cross-check: US general guidance puts an interior plant maintenance program at
**$100 to $600 per month**. — https://goodearthplants.com/faq/how-much-do-office-plants-cost/

Sanity check against DE: Reiffer's **€9/plant/month** floor is far below the US
**$20–35** floor. Either German rental is genuinely cheaper, or the €9 plant is small.
I could not resolve this; flag as an open question.

## 4. UAE / Dubai

- Plant rental services exist and explicitly market to **offices, homes and events**:
  Adplants ("offices, homes and events", "fresh plants, quick delivery and hassle-free
  maintenance") — https://adplants.com/pages/plant-rentals ;
  Desert Blooms ("Stylish Green Solutions for Homes & Office") —
  https://www.desertblooms.me/pages/plant-rental-landscape-dubai ;
  Plantsworld — https://plantsworld.ae/collections/office-plant-rental ;
  Hope Plants — https://hopeplantsdubai.com/indoor-plant-hire ;
  Plants Xpert — https://plantsxpert.com/pages/plants-rental-in-dubai-plants-xpert
- **Not one of them publishes a price.** Every single one routes to WhatsApp, phone, or a
  "free site assessment." I fetched Plantsworld and Adplants directly and confirmed zero
  rental rates on page (2026-09-10).
- 800petals is the most explicit about structure without giving numbers: monthly fee
  covers plants, pots, **weekly professional maintenance**, and **free replacement of
  declining plants**; "most plans quoted **per plant per month** on an all-inclusive
  basis"; "pricing depends on plant count, sizes and pot finishes"; **most clients choose
  12-month agreements**; quote within 24h after free assessment. Service areas listed are
  commercial districts (DIFC, Business Bay, Media City, JLT, Downtown) — **homes/villas
  are not mentioned**.
  — https://800petals.com/office-plant-rental-dubai/ (2026)
- Note the cadence difference: Dubai operators advertise **weekly** maintenance; German
  operators do **every 2–3 weeks**. Consistent with a hotter, drier, more air-conditioned
  environment. That is a real cost-per-account difference of ~2–3x in visit labour.
- On the **retail** side UAE is well served and price-transparent: Plntd sells plants
  **AED 154 (Peace Lily) to AED 1,199 (Bird of Paradise)**, free shipping over AED 100,
  next-day ship, 14-day money-back guarantee, and claims "Over **60,000 homes** across
  Dubai, Abu Dhabi, and the UAE trust us." Plntd does **not** offer rental or subscription.
  — https://plntd.ae/ (2026)
- Adplants: free shipping over **AED 250**; ACE UAE: free delivery over **AED 99**.
  — https://adplants.com/ , https://www.aceuae.com/en-ae/outdoor-and-garden/potting-and-planting/plant/indoor-plants/

**Lesson:** in Dubai there is a functioning rental *supply* industry that is entirely
offline/WhatsApp-mediated and quote-only, plus a price-transparent retail e-commerce
layer with a demonstrated 60k-home consumer base. The gap between those two — an
online, instantly-priced, self-serve rental checkout — is unoccupied in both markets, but
the *retail* proof of consumer demand is stronger in the UAE than anything I found for
German home plant subscriptions.

## 5. Churn and retention signals

Hard, company-specific churn numbers for plant subscriptions are **not public**. I found
none for Horti, The Sill, Bloomscape, Patch or Plantclub. What I did find:

- Subbly's 2024 subscription-churn report: **median churn 7.44%/month** across all
  merchants and models, and "subscription boxes demonstrate the highest churn among
  larger businesses."
  — https://www.subbly.co/blog/subscription-churn-data-report/ (2024)
- Broader benchmarks (secondary, aggregator quality — treat as directional):
  average subscription churn **~5.3%/month**; top performers **below 3%**; meal kits
  **12.7%** (highest of the categories listed); replenishment models **below 4%**;
  **annual-billing subscribers churn 51% less** than monthly and are **2.4x more
  profitable**; merchants offering a pause option saw **39.7% adoption**, preventing
  400,000+ cancellations.
  — https://churnfree.com/blog/average-churn-rate-for-subscription-services/
  — https://www.swell.is/content/subscription-box-statistics

At 7.4%/month churn, average customer life is ~13.5 months (1/0.074, my calculation).
For a rental business that has to *physically recover and refurbish* the plant, that is
the number that decides whether the asset ever pays back.

**Behavioural churn causes I could evidence:**
1. **Saturation / no space** — Lively Root's move to quarterly cadence and their own copy
   about not "overwhelming recipients." (https://www.livelyroot.com/pages/lively-root-subscriptions)
2. **Price-anchoring against retail** — the Horti reviewer's "$12 for two" at the grocery
   store vs $28/month. (https://www.mysubscriptionaddiction.com/b/horti)
3. **Plant arrives dead/damaged** — dominant complaint theme at Bloomscape (2.8/5),
   Bloombox Club (1.8/5), and even in The Sill's 17% 1-star tail.
4. **Refund friction** — replacement-or-credit-not-cash policies generate 1-star reviews.
5. **Cancellation friction** — "it's impossible to log into their website!"
   (https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/bloomboxclub.com)

## 6. What breaks logistically (synthesis)

- **Parcel shipping kills plants.** Bloomscape 2.8/5 vs Patch 4.7/5 is the evidence.
  The Sill's own customers: "these should not be sent as a ground shipment" (2026).
- **Route density is the whole game.** Reflower restricts to 3 provinces and delivers on
  Wednesdays only. Plantclub sells by **m² of office**, not per plant, so a single visit
  services a large revenue base. Reiffer imposes a **€75/month minimum order** and a
  **2-year term** precisely so a truck roll is worth making.
  — https://objektbepflanzung.de/pflanzen-vermietung
- **Visit labour dominates.** Every 3 weeks (DE) vs weekly (UAE) is a 3x labour swing on
  the same revenue. Generic rental-business commentary makes the same point: "low rates
  can leave owners paying for swaps and extra visits out of pocket."
  — https://www.newprocontainers.com/blog/start-profitable-plant-rental-side-business/
- **Local physical rental fragments the market.** Commentary on rental marketplaces:
  "Logistics costs for local pickups, transportation, or packaging quickly make the model
  unprofitable without large-scale organic demand" and physical rentals are "inherently
  local."
  — https://www.twicecommerce.com/studios/episodes/why-equipment-rental-marketplaces-fail----and-how-professional-supply-can-fix-it
- **Plant rental has a high infant-mortality rate as a business**: "Plant rental and
  leasing business startups aren't easy - many fail within the first year."
  — https://www.gaebler.com/Opening-a-Plant-Rental-and-Leasing-Business.htm (undated;
  anecdotal/low confidence)

## 7. Implications for Planty (argued)

1. **The B2C-home-rental thesis is the weakest part of the idea, and every dead company
   in this lens died on the B2C side.** Horti (closed 2025), Botanicly (insolvent 2024),
   The Plant Box (insolvent 2023), Bloombox Club (1.8/5, site down 2026), Bloomscape
   (2.8/5, review volume collapsed). Meanwhile the B2B rental incumbents are boring,
   quote-only, and durable.
2. **Lead with B2B; keep B2C as a same-route add-on.** Plantclub proves a Berlin B2B
   plants-as-a-service business is buildable by a small team with published pricing and
   bi-weekly visits, and got to ~200 members across 7 cities. Planty can only beat that
   by being *better at one city* — and by opening the B2C-in-the-same-postcode segment
   that Plantclub explicitly does not serve.
3. **Price B2C rental at €25–45/plant-set/month, not €9/plant.** Reflower's €30 (L) /
   €45 (XL) is the only clean consumer comparable, and it works with monthly cancellation.
   Below ~€25 the truck roll cannot be paid for.
4. **Impose a minimum monthly spend, not a minimum plant count.** Reiffer's €75/month
   minimum and officeplants.com's $100–200 minimum both exist for the same reason. My
   recommendation: a €69–99/month floor for homes in the MVP.
5. **The rotate/swap mechanic is Planty's genuine edge over subscription boxes** — because
   it solves the #1 evidenced churn cause (saturation) that forced Lively Root to
   quarterly cadence. A swap keeps the customer's plant count constant. Say this loudly
   in the marketing.
6. **Weekly visits are not affordable for B2C at these price points.** German B2B runs
   every 2–3 weeks at €199–299/month. Planty's stated "every week" cadence should become
   **every 2–3 weeks for B2C** and weekly only as a paid add-on tier or in the UAE where
   it is the market norm.
7. **Publish prices with instant checkout.** Not one German and not one Dubai rental
   competitor publishes a rental price online. Plantclub is the sole exception, and it is
   the only one that looks like a startup. For a solo technical founder shipping
   Next.js + Supabase, "the only plant rental you can actually buy at 11pm without talking
   to a salesperson" is a defensible, cheap-to-build wedge in either market.
8. **Market pick, on this lens's evidence only:** Dubai edges Berlin. Berlin's consumer
   plant market has an active graveyard (Botanicly, The Plant Box) and an entrenched
   B2B incumbent (Plantclub) with logos like Netflix and Figma. Dubai has a
   price-transparent retail layer proving 60k+ homes buy plants online, an entirely
   quote-only/WhatsApp rental supply side with no digital competitor, and cultural fit
   ("villas", serviced housing, high expat turnover, outsourced-household-services norm).
   The cost against it: weekly visit cadence is the Dubai norm, which is 2–3x the labour
   of the German cadence, and the founder is not physically in Dubai. **This lens cannot
   settle the market question alone** — it should be weighed against the demand-side and
   ops-cost lenses.

- Reiffer's **€9/plant/month** vs US **$20–35/plant/month** — is German rental genuinely

- Plantclub's revenue is unpublished; the "200 members" figure came from a search snippet
  whose source URL now 404s.
- No UAE rental operator publishes a price, so Dubai's actual per-plant-per-month rate is

- Bergamotte (FR): search surfaced two *unrelated* small companies named
  Bergamotte/Bergamote in judicial liquidation (Cognac, Hardinghen). The e-commerce
