## 1. How the trade actually runs "seasonal colour" programmes

The interior-landscaping industry separates two product classes:

1. **Permanent foliage** — acclimatised, slow-growing, kept for years and maintained on a visit route.
2. **Blooming/colour rotations** — greenhouse-budded flowering plants placed for the length of their bloom and then swapped out. They are a separate line item and a separate logistics loop.

Evidence:

- Planterra Lighting Guide (2019, US interior landscaper): "Flowers can, however, function as a significant component of interior landscape designs, specified as color rotations. These flowering plants are grown and budded in greenhouses and sent to the site location to be displayed only for the duration of the bloom cycle. Common examples include the orchid, bromeliad, azalea and the chrysanthemum." It also states rotation plants "do not need to be factored into the lighting design, except for decorative considerations." Source: https://planterra.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/PlanterraLightingGuide4.19.pdf (2019)
- Ambius (US, Rentokil group): blooming rotation service "available in 2 to 6 week rotations"; plants named: bromeliads, orchids, mums, seasonal poinsettias. Source (search snippet of): https://www.ambius.com/ambius-catalogs/blooming-plants/ (accessed 2026; page itself returns 403 to fetch)
- Foliage Services (Portland, US): interior blooming rotation "every 3–12 weeks", exterior 1–3x/yr; species azaleas, African violets, begonias, bromeliads, chrysanthemums, poinsettias, orchids. Source: https://www.foliage-services.com/blooming-rotation/1891854 (accessed 2026)
- Green Thumb Interior (Orlando, US) rotation catalog: bromeliads (Guzmania 'Samba', 'Luna', Aechmea fasciata, Neoregelia, Vriesea 'Poelmanii', V. splendens), crotons, Spathiphyllum 'Figaro', anthurium red/pink, poinsettia (red, pink, white, novelty), azalea, chrysanthemum, kalanchoe. Quote: "We regularly swap out fresh blooming plants, bromeliads, and colorful foliage." Source: https://www.greenthumbinterior.com/blooming-plant-rotations-catalog/ (accessed 2026)
- Natural Surroundings (California): bromeliad rotation "every two months (six times annually)"; seasonal rotation list "lilies, mums, poinsettias, hydrangeas, kalanchoes, bromeliads". Source: https://naturalsurroundings.us/orchids-seasonal-flowers/ (accessed 2026)
- officeplants.com (US, Aug 2026): "Plants on rotation schedules are flowering varieties like bromeliads and orchids that are maintained for shorter periods than other varieties." Also: "Monthly maintenance should not be assumed… Weekly or bi-weekly care may be appropriate in some commercial settings." Source: https://officeplants.com/2026/08/28/how-to-choose-an-interior-landscaping-company/ (2026)

**Interpretation for Planty.** "Fresh, seasonal" in the trade means: a small set of flowering "colour" units on a 4–8-week swap cycle, layered on top of a permanent foliage set that is *not* rotated (only replaced on decline). Nobody rotates the foliage backbone seasonally — it would be uneconomic (acclimatised specimens take 3–18 months to condition, see §5) and unnecessary. Planty's marketing promise should be mapped onto this structure.

### Bloom life of the standard rotation plants (how long a "colour unit" earns its keep)

- Phalaenopsis: "most blooms typically last from two to three months"; each flower 80–120 days (Just Add Ice). Source: https://www.justaddiceorchids.com/just-add-ice-orchid-blog/bid/92957/the-life-cycle-of-a-phalaenopsis-orchid ; NYBG: https://libanswers.nybg.org/faq/223105 (accessed 2026). Dubai-specific: "A single stem can flower for 8 to 12 weeks" — https://upscaleandposh.com/blogs/news/indoor-plants-in-dubai-the-2026-guide-to-greenery-that-thrives (2026)
- Guzmania bromeliad: blooms 2–4 months (conservative) to 3–6 months; monocarpic (flowers once, then declines over 6–12 months). Sources: https://www.joyusgarden.com/guzmania-bromeliad-plant-care-tips/ , https://help.costafarms.com/en/knowledge/how-does-a-bromeliad-grow-and-bloom (accessed 2026). Ambius UK: tank-forming bromeliads should have the central cup emptied/refilled every 2–4 weeks — https://www.ambius.co.uk/plant-guides/bromeliads-plant/ (accessed 2026)
- Spathiphyllum: white spathes "several times a year and last 4 to 6 weeks each" — https://upscaleandposh.com/blogs/news/indoor-plants-in-dubai-the-2026-guide-to-greenery-that-thrives (2026)
- Cyclamen (florist, C. persicum): flowers longest at 10–15 °C; "the moment the temperature consistently goes above 15 degrees, the plant starts to decline: flowers fade quickly, leaves yellow" — https://www.gardenersworld.com/house-plants/how-to-grow-indoor-cyclamen/ and https://greg.app/cyclamen-temperature/ (accessed 2026). **This rules cyclamen out for Dubai AC interiors (18–22 °C) and most heated Berlin offices; only cool lobbies/stairwells.**
- Poinsettia: abscission triggered below ~13 °C (55 °F); "poinsettias can drop leaves fast after even minutes below 50°F (10°C)"; leaf drop within 24–72 h and irreversible. Sources: https://www.gardeningknowhow.com/ornamental/poinsettia/poinsettia-cold-shock-instant-killer , https://gardenerspath.com/plants/houseplants/poinsettia-cold-tolerance/ (accessed 2026). **Berlin December deliveries need sleeved plants and a heated van; Dubai has the opposite problem (hot van) — see §4.**

## 2. Reference light levels and species-by-light lists (Planterra, 2019)

Planterra Lighting Guide (2019), based on acclimatised foliage receiving 12 h light/day:

- Low-light tolerant (50–100 fc ≈ 540–1,080 lux): Aspidistra elatior, Aglaonema, Philodendron cordatum, Dracaena 'Janet Craig', D. 'Janet Craig Compacta', D. fragrans 'Massangeana', Spathiphyllum, Epipremnum, Sansevieria, Zamioculcas.
- Medium (100–250 fc): Schefflera arboricola, Hedera canariensis, Chamaedorea erumpens, Asplenium nidus, Dracaena marginata, Howea forsteriana (Kentia), Rhapis excelsa, Dracaena reflexa, Ficus elastica, Chlorophytum, Dracaena 'Warneckii'.
- High (250+ fc): Ficus benjamina, Dypsis lutescens (Areca), Strelitzia, Ficus lyrata, Beaucarnea, Phoenix roebelenii, Codiaeum (croton), Cycas, Yucca, Crassula.
- Minimums: "Office with low-light tolerant plants > 75 fc; Living wall > 150 fc; Atrium with trees > 250 fc"; supplemental light to ensure 10–12 h/day; "Foot-candles in interior commercial spaces range between 50 fc [and] …". 1 fc = 10.76 lux.
- Acclimatisation: "Depending on the size and type of plant, acclimatization can take three to 18 months… Acclimatized foliage is costlier, but failure to acclimatize plants typically leads to… defoliation, higher susceptibility to disease and pests, and plant death." Tall trees: reserve a year ahead.
Source: https://planterra.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/PlanterraLightingGuide4.19.pdf (2019)

Other light references: Dracaena needs 200–400 fc to hold variegation long-term (https://intuitiveplants.org/blogs/houseplants-light-101/dracaena-light-needs-unveiled-a-comprehensive-guide , accessed 2026); Aglaonema 50–250 fc, tolerates as low as 10 fc (https://lifetips.alibaba.com/plant-care/aglaonema-light , accessed 2026); general: "less than 50 foot-candles will not sustain plant life for very long" (https://extension.msstate.edu/publications/care-selection-indoor-plants , accessed 2026).

## 3. Berlin conditions (winter dark, heated dry air)

- Berlin sunshine (DWD via GASAG): December 2023 = 30 h (1.0 h/day); January 2024 = 65 h (2.1 h/day); June 2023 = 265 h; annual 2025 = 1,635 h. Source: https://www.gasag.de/magazin/erneuerbare-energien/sonnenstunden-in-berlin/ (data 2023–2025)
- Implication (EST): November–February interior light in Berlin will often be below 50–75 fc more than 1 m from a window; only the Planterra low-light list is safe in winter; Areca/Ficus lyrata/Strelitzia will decline in Berlin winter without supplemental light. Heating drops relative humidity → spider-mite pressure (see §6).
- German trade uses hydroculture (Hydrokultur/LECA with water-level gauge) almost universally for rental: no soil → no fungus gnats/mould; care intervals plannable; plants "bleiben in der Regel 10 bis 20 Jahre gesund und vital" vs soil plants replaced after "3 bis 6 Jahren". Sources: https://greenmeup.de/pages/hydrokulturpflanzen-fur-dein-buro , https://www.gaertner-gregg.de/buerobegruenung-mit-hydrokultur/ , https://kremkau.de/hydrokultur-pflanzen/ (accessed 2026)
- German rental service cadence: Gärtner Gregg "etwa alle vier Wochen"; Kinnula "alle zwei bis vier Wochen"; Hydro Lesser "alle zwei bis vier Wochen", 24-month term. Sources: https://www.gaertner-gregg.de/mietpflanzen-muenster-dortmund/ , https://hydro-kulturen.de/pflanzen-mieten/ , https://www.hydro-lesser.de/mietservice.html (accessed 2026)
- German seasonal potted-plant calendar (nursery/retail sources): spring primroses, hyacinths, tulips, ranunculus; summer geraniums, petunias, begonias, gerbera, celosia; autumn asters, chrysanthemums, hydrangeas, heather; winter amaryllis, Christmas rose, hyacinths, cyclamen (Alpenveilchen), poinsettia. Sources: https://www.gaertnerei-ehrmann.de/saisonpflanzen/ , https://www.fleurop.de/blog/bluehkalender (accessed 2026)
- German market ranking (AMI/ZVG): orchids top the flowering-houseplant top-10, followed by poinsettias and roses (2024). Source: https://www.derdeutschegartenbau.de/2025/01/27/preisentwicklung-laesst-markt-wachsen/ (2024 data, published Jan 2025)

## 6. Pests and failure modes relevant to a rental fleet

- "Mealybugs are one of the primary insect pests of interior plantscapes and are problems… because the plants are generally present for a long period of time." Spider mites "thrive in warm, dry environments" (heated Berlin winters, Dubai AC). Fungus gnats need constantly moist, organic soil. Sources: https://agsci.colostate.edu/agbio/ipm-pests/houseplant-pests/ , https://extension.missouri.edu/publications/g7273 , https://www.ourhouseplants.com/guides/house-plant-pests (accessed 2026)
- Hydroculture eliminates fungus gnats and mould ("weder Schimmel noch… Trauermücken") — https://greenmeup.de/pages/hydrokulturpflanzen-fur-dein-buro (accessed 2026)
- Ficus benjamina "is notorious for dropping leaves when stressed or exposed to environmental changes"; Ficus lyrata "can be fussy… needs bright but indirect sunlight… consistently moist soil and high humidity". Sources: https://plantdetectives.com/pages/the-ficus-guide , https://gardenerspath.com/plants/houseplants/grow-fiddle-leaf-fig/ (accessed 2026)
- No public benchmark for plants-per-technician-hour was found; the one operator blog on the topic defines productivity as onsite hours ÷ paid hours and warns "unbillable hours were probably spent driving, parking, loading, unloading" — https://www.newprocontainers.com/blog/measuring-your-plant-techs-productivity-efficiency-quality-of-work/ (accessed 2026)

## 9. Twelve-month colour-rotation calendar, viable in both Berlin and Dubai

Design rule (derived from §1): **always-on trio** (Phalaenopsis, Bromeliad, Anthurium — all available 52 weeks from Dutch/UAE supply, all tolerate 18–24 °C interiors) + **one true seasonal accent per period**. Swap cadence: colour units every 4–8 weeks (Ambius 2–6 wk; Foliage Services 3–12 wk; Natural Surroundings bromeliads every 8 wk). Foliage never rotates seasonally; it is replaced on decline.

## 10. Plants to avoid in a rental catalog (with reason)

- Ficus benjamina (weeping fig): leaf drop on any move/draught — every install is a move. (Plant Detectives, 2026)
- Ficus lyrata (fiddle-leaf): high light + humidity + consistency; fails in AC and in Berlin winter; large-SKU capital at risk. (Gardener's Path, 2026)
- Calathea / Maranta / Ctenanthe: need 60%+ RH; crisp in 30–40% AC air and heated Berlin flats (Acacia 2026 humidity data). Pet-safe but not rentable.
- Boston fern, Asplenium in AC: same humidity problem.
- Croton (Codiaeum): H light, drops leaves with draughts, spider-mite magnet.
- Hedera helix, Ivy: spider mites in dry interiors.
- Dieffenbachia: strongest oxalate irritant; child/pet liability; skip entirely.
- Azalea, Kalanchoe, Cyclamen, lilies for pet households: cardiac/renal toxins (ASPCA). Keep azalea and true lilies out of the catalog altogether; gate kalanchoe/cyclamen behind a "no pets" flag.
- Cyclamen anywhere above ~15–18 °C; Areca/Strelitzia in Berlin Nov–Feb; anything on the Planterra "high light" list in windowless offices.
- Cacti/yucca/agave/Euphorbia tirucalli: spines and caustic sap — injury risk for technicians and children.
- Soil-grown stock generally: fungus gnats and inconsistent watering; adopt hydro/semi-hydro (LECA + water gauge) as German rental firms do, at least for L/XL foliage.
