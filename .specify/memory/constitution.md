# Planty Constitution

## Core Principles

### I. Route Density Is the Business (NON-NEGOTIABLE)

Planty sells recurring physical visits. The cost of a visit is dominated by
travel, not by the plants. Every product and technical decision MUST protect
the number of paying stops a technician completes per day:

- Service coverage is a **hard-bounded set of named zones**. An address
  outside a served zone cannot check out — it joins a waitlist. No silent
  expansion, no "we'll figure out the drive".
- Every subscription MUST meet a **minimum monthly value** for its zone. A
  basket below that minimum is not an order.
- Visit scheduling is **zone-day based**: a customer picks from the days
  Planty already serves their zone, never an arbitrary date.
- Any feature that increases kilometres per euro of revenue must justify
  itself in its spec against this principle, in writing.

*Rationale*: The failure post-mortems of comparable rental businesses
(furniture, bikes, cars) all reduce to cost-per-stop exceeding revenue-per-stop.
This is the one constraint that kills the company.

### II. B2B-First, B2C Only On An Existing Route

Offices, coworking spaces, clinics, hotels and showrooms are the primary
customer. They buy 10–40 plants at one address, sign longer terms, and make
a stop worth making.

- Every release MUST serve the B2B buyer. B2C features ship only when they
  reuse B2B machinery already built.
- B2C addresses are accepted only in zones where a B2B route already runs.
- We do not build B2C-only infrastructure (consumer marketplaces, gifting,
  social features) in the MVP.

*Rationale*: A home basket is a fraction of an office basket at a similar
visit cost. B2C is a margin experiment layered on a B2B route, not a
standalone business.

### III. Transparent Self-Serve Pricing

No competitor found in any researched market publishes rental prices with an
online checkout. That opacity is the wedge.

- Every plant, bundle and add-on MUST show its price without a sales call,
  without a form, and without an account.
- The price a customer sees when configuring MUST equal the price they are
  charged. Quote-only pricing, "from" prices that don't resolve, and hidden
  travel or setup surcharges are prohibited.
- A customer MUST be able to go from landing page to confirmed subscription
  without a human in the loop. A "talk to us" path may exist alongside it,
  never instead of it.

### IV. Proof of Service

The customer is buying an outcome they cannot easily verify. Planty MUST make
each visit provable.

- Every completed visit records: timestamp, technician, per-plant condition,
  actions taken, and at least one photo.
- The customer can see that record for their own site, always, without asking.
- A visit is not "done" until its record is complete. No back-dated or
  photo-less completions.

### V. Honest Plant Promises

"Fresh, seasonal plants" MUST mean a defined, bounded entitlement, not an
open-ended promise:

- The permanent foliage set is replaced **on decline**, not on a schedule.
- Seasonal variety is delivered as a **counted rotation entitlement** per
  billing period, shown in the product and consumed when used.
- Replacement guarantees MUST state their exclusions in the customer's own
  contract view (damage by others, blocked access, moved plants, climate
  outside agreed range).
- We never display a plant as available when no healthy unit exists in stock.

### VI. Supabase-Native Backend

Postgres with Row Level Security is the source of truth.

- Supabase Auth, Postgres, Storage, Realtime and Edge Functions come before
  any custom infrastructure.
- **Every table has RLS policies before it ships.** No service-key-only
  security.
- Logic that must be trusted — pricing, subscription state, visit state,
  asset allocation — lives in the database (constraints, triggers, security
  definer functions), never only in the client.

### VII. Simplicity First (NON-NEGOTIABLE)

Build only what the current spec requires. No speculative abstractions, no
configurability nobody asked for, no premature multi-city or multi-currency
architecture. One market, one currency, English-first, until a spec says
otherwise. If a 200-line screen could be 50, rewrite it.

### VIII. Spec-Driven Development

All non-trivial work flows through Spec Kit: specify → clarify → plan →
tasks → implement. Specs define WHAT and WHY with testable acceptance
criteria; plans define HOW. Code that does not trace to a spec requirement is
scope creep.

### IX. Test What Matters

These paths MUST have automated tests before merge, because money and trust
depend on them:

- Pricing computation (per-plant rates, term multipliers, bundle totals,
  proration).
- Subscription and visit state transitions.
- Asset allocation (a physical plant is never in two places).
- RLS policies for every table, including cross-tenant isolation for B2B
  organizations.

UI and glue code are tested pragmatically, not dogmatically.

## Market & Operating Constraints

- **Launch market**: Dubai, UAE. Currency **AED**. Timezone **Asia/Dubai**
  (GST, no DST). Working week Mon–Fri, with Fri afternoon and Sat–Sun
  treated as reduced-service. This choice rests on evidence recorded in
  `docs/research/` and on one unresolved founder-side question recorded in
  `docs/PRD.md` §Open Questions; changing the market requires an amendment.
- **Language**: English-first UI. Arabic/RTL is a future spec — avoid
  hardcoding that makes RTL painful (use logical CSS properties). UAE
  consumer-facing contract documents may require Arabic before commercial
  launch; that is a launch-blocker item, not an MVP feature.
- **Visit cadence**: weekly is the Dubai market norm and the default.
  Lower cadences are priced tiers, never silent downgrades.
- **Plant care**: no chemical plant-protection products in the MVP.
  Mechanical and biological control plus swap-out only.
- **Payments**: card-on-file via a single payment provider for recurring
  B2C; invoice-based collection for B2B. No cash handling, no marketplace
  payouts, no split payments.
- **Privacy**: customer addresses, access codes and site photos are visible
  only to that customer and to Planty staff assigned to the visit. Access
  codes and keys are never shown to a technician outside an assigned visit
  window.
- **Scale assumption**: pilot scale — one city cluster, low tens of business
  accounts, 1–2 technicians. Architecture that only pays off above that is
  premature.

## Development Workflow

- One spec-kit feature per branch, numbered sequentially under `specs/`.
- Database changes ship as numbered SQL migrations with their RLS policies
  and pgTAP tests in the same change.
- `npm run build`, `npm run test` and `supabase test db` must be green before
  a feature is merged.
- The agent context file (`CLAUDE.md`) is regenerated by the plan phase, not
  hand-edited outside its manual-additions markers.

## Governance

This constitution supersedes ad-hoc practices. Any spec, plan, or pull
request that conflicts with a principle must either change, or carry a
written and justified amendment to this document recorded in the plan's
Complexity Tracking table.

Amendments require a version bump and a note in the spec that prompted them.
Versioning is semantic: MAJOR for removing or redefining a principle, MINOR
for adding one or materially expanding guidance, PATCH for clarifications.
